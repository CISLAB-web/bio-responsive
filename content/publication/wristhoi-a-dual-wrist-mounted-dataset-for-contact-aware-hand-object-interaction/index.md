  Interaction"

abstract: Fine-grained hand-object interaction (HOI) understanding relies on the
  accurate perception of contact information. However, observations from
  conventional allocentric and egocentric viewpoints are often severely affected
  by hand-object occlusion, resulting in sparse visual cues around critical
  contact regions. We introduce WristHOI, a contact-centric diagnostic benchmark
  designed to systematically investigate local occlusion in near-field HOI. The
  benchmark provides synchronized dorsal and palmar wrist-mounted RGB-D
  observations, enabling the analysis of view-dependent contact observability
  and multi-view fusion under extreme perspective shifts. Comprising 1,260
  interaction sequences from 10 subjects manipulating 14 rigid objects under
  three grasping paradigms, the dataset totals approximately 1.45M frames. It
  provides multi-level annotations, including MANO-based hand geometry, object
  6D poses, contact labels, and temporal interaction states, whose reliability
  is systematically assessed through quantitative validation. Benchmark results
  highlight the perceptual challenges arising from asymmetric view observability
  in near-field HOI. We demonstrate that while dual-view sensing provides
  additional proximal observations, its effectiveness depends strongly on
  adaptive multi-view fusion strategies. Overall, WristHOI serves as a
  challenging testbed for studying contact observability and multi-view HOI
  under near-field occlusion. The dataset and code will be publicly released
  upon acceptance.
